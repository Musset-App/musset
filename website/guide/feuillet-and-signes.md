## Signes and feuillets

A French publisher does not ask how many words your novel has. The French unit is the **signe**: one character, and a space counts as one. Length is given in *signes espaces comprises*, characters with spaces.

The **feuillet** is the page that goes with it. One feuillet is **1,500 signes**, by convention 25 lines of 60 characters. It is the unit of the press, of translation and of many publishing contracts, and it lets everyone speak of pages without arguing about layout.

| Unit | Is |
|---|---|
| 1 signe | One character, spaces included |
| 1 feuillet | 1,500 signes |
| A novel of 300 feuillets | 450,000 signes |

To convert from an English habit: French prose runs at about six signes per word with its space, so a feuillet is around 250 words, and 450,000 signes is a novel of roughly 75,000 words.

## Count in signes while you write

Click the count at the bottom of the page until it reads **characters with spaces**. The whole app then counts that way: each chapter, the book, the sidebar.

Musset leaves out of the count what is not the text: your notes to yourself, scene titles and Markdown marks.

## What a French manuscript looks like

French houses are less strict about the page than American ones, and many now read on screen. The shared expectations are simple:

| Element | Convention |
|---|---|
| Paper | A4, single-sided, pages numbered |
| Type | A readable face at 12 point |
| Line spacing | Double, or 1.5 |
| Margins | About 2.5 cm, enough to annotate |
| Paragraphs | First line indented, the first of a chapter too |
| Title page | Title, your name, your contact details, the length in signes |

## Make one in Musset

1. In the book's settings, set the **Book language** to **Français**, and enter your **Contact (manuscript)**.
2. Press [[⌘E]] and choose **PDF**.
3. Choose the layout **Manuscript for French publishers**.
4. Click **Export**.

The page is A4 with 2.5 cm margins, set at 12 point with double spacing, aligned on the left. The header carries your surname and the title, with the page number on the right.

The title page gives the length as publishers read it: *329 feuillets (493 000 signes)*. The number of feuillets is the count of signes divided by 1,500.

## French typography, set for you

A French manuscript is judged on its typography sooner than an English one. Musset applies the rules as you write:

- « guillemets » with a non-breaking space inside each;
- a non-breaking space before `;` `:` `!` and `?`, so that no line begins with a lone mark;
- the long dash for dialogue, at the start of the line;
- the curly apostrophe, and the ellipsis as one character.

The spaces are the standard non-breaking space, which every typesetter and every version of Word understands. See [automatic typography](/guide/smart-typography/).

## Submitting to a prize

Most French prizes for unpublished manuscripts are judged blind. In the export sheet, fill in **Pseudonym (prizes)**: the title page and the header carry the pseudonym, and your contact details are left out. Rename the exported file before sending it, since its name contains the author's name from the book's settings.

## Common questions

**Signes with or without spaces?** With, unless told otherwise. *Espaces comprises* is the default in French publishing.

**How long is a first novel?** Houses commonly receive novels between 200,000 and 600,000 signes. The house's own submission page is the only rule that counts.

**Is the feuillet the same as a page of my manuscript?** No. The feuillet is a unit of 1,500 signes. Your manuscript's pages hold a different amount, depending on the type and the dialogue.

**And the calibrage?** *Calibrer* a text is to measure it in signes so as to know how many printed pages it will make. The count Musset prints on the title page is that measurement.

Other countries have their own units: see [manuscript formats by country](/guide/manuscript-formats-by-country/).
