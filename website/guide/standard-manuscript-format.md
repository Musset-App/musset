## What standard manuscript format is

Standard manuscript format is the plain, uniform layout that English-language agents, editors and magazines expect for fiction. It has no designer and no official body; it is a set of conventions, grown out of the typewriter era and kept because they work.

The point is the reader of the slush pile. A uniform page lets them judge length at a glance, read for hours without strain, and write between the lines. A manuscript that ignores the conventions is not rejected for it, but it starts by showing that its author did not look them up.

## The rules

| Element | Convention |
|---|---|
| Paper | US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) in North America, A4 elsewhere |
| Margins | One inch (2.54 cm) all round |
| Typeface | 12 point, Times New Roman or Courier |
| Line spacing | Double |
| Alignment | Left, with a ragged right edge; no hyphenation |
| Paragraphs | First line indented half an inch; no blank line between paragraphs |
| Header | Surname / TITLE / page number, top right, from page two |
| Chapters | Each on a new page, starting about a third of the way down |
| Scene breaks | A centred `#` on a line of its own |
| Italics | Set as italics |
| Pages | Single-sided |

## The first page

The title page carries what an editor needs to file the manuscript and to answer you:

- **top left**: your legal name and your contact details;
- **top right**: the word count, rounded to the nearest hundred, as in *about 82,400 words*;
- **centre of the page**: the title, and under it the name you publish under.

Round the count. An exact figure such as 82,417 words reads as fussy; publishers budget in hundreds and thousands.

## Make one in Musset

1. In the book's settings, check that the **Book language** is English (US) or English (UK), and enter your **Contact (manuscript)**.
2. Press [[⌘E]] and choose **PDF**.
3. Choose the layout **Standard manuscript (US Letter)** or **Standard manuscript (A4)**.
4. Choose what goes in: the whole book, or **Chosen chapters** for a partial.
5. Click **Export**.

Musset sets the page, the header, the title page and the rounded word count. Chapters start on a new page, a third of the way down, numbered above their titles.

A few details of how Musset handles it:

- The typeface is Times New Roman.
- A scene break you typed as `* * *` prints as a centred `#`. Between Musset's own scenes, what prints follows the book's **Between scenes** setting: choose *Blank line* or *⁂* in the export sheet.
- Notes to yourself, margin notes and undecided corrections are never in the export.

## Sending the first three chapters

Agents usually ask for a sample: the first three chapters, the first fifty pages or the first ten thousand words. In the export sheet choose **Chosen chapters** and tick them. The word count on the title page is that of what you send; state the length of the whole book in your query letter.

To see how many pages a selection makes, look at the estimate at the bottom of the export sheet.

## UK and US differences

The format is the same; the page and the punctuation differ. A British manuscript is on A4 and uses single quotation marks with double inside; an American one is on Letter with double quotation marks and unspaced em dashes. Musset follows the book's language for both. See [automatic typography](/guide/smart-typography/).

## Common questions

**Courier or Times New Roman?** Either is accepted almost everywhere today. Courier is the older habit; Times New Roman is now the more common request.

**One space or two after a full stop?** One.

**Should I add "THE END"?** Many writers type *END* or *THE END* centred after the last line. Add it as the last line of your last chapter if you want it.

**Do I need a cover?** No. A manuscript has a title page, not a cover.

**Does it apply to short stories?** Yes; the same format is used for magazine submissions, with the story starting on the first page under the title.

**What if the guidelines differ?** Follow the guidelines. This format is what to send when there are none.
