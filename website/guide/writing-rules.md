## Your rules, not a style guide's

Every writer ends up with a private style sheet. *No adverbs on dialogue tags. Never "suddenly". Numbers in words up to a hundred. The narrator never names the town. One simile a page at most.* Publishers have house rules of the same kind.

A grammar checker cannot enforce them, because they are yours. A **checker** in Musset is a set of rules you write, which the AI then applies to a chapter, flagging each breach in the margin.

## Write a checker

1. Open the AI Reader panel ([[⌘3]]), open its menu and choose **Checkers…**. They are also in the book's settings, under **AI reader**.
2. Click **+** for a new checker and name it: *My style sheet*, *House rules*, *Dialogue*.
3. Write the rules as you would give them to an editor, and **number them**.

```
1. No adverb on a dialogue tag. "said" is enough.
2. Never "suddenly", "began to", "seemed to".
3. Numbers in words up to one hundred, except dates.
4. The town is never named.
5. No more than one simile per page.
```

Plain sentences work best. You can also say how you want the findings presented.

## Run it on a chapter

Tick the checkers this book uses. Each one then appears beside the readings in the AI Reader panel, and in the command bar ([[⌘K]]). Click it with a chapter open.

## What you get

A note in the margin on each breach, beginning with **the number of the rule**, then the reason, then a few leads to think from. The leads are prompts, not replacements: Musset never applies what the checker writes, and the checker is told to flag, never to rewrite.

Two refinements make it livable:

- A breach that looks **deliberate** comes back as a question, not a fault. Rules are broken on purpose.
- Something outside your rules that the reader happens to notice, a typo, a doubtful fact, is added apart, marked *Outside the rules*.

Run the checker again after revising: its earlier notes on the chapter are replaced, so the margin shows what is still true.

Work through the notes as with any others: tick, resolve, reply. See [notes and to-dos](/guide/notes-and-todos/).

## One library for all your books

Checkers are kept in a library on your Mac and shared by all your books: write your style sheet once. Each book ticks the ones it uses and carries a copy, so the rules travel with the book to another Mac.

A series can have its own checker: the spelling of invented names, the rules of its magic, what the narrator of this series never does.

## Ideas for checkers

- **A publisher's house style**, pasted from their guidelines.
- **Point of view**: *the narrator knows only what Marta sees, hears or remembers.*
- **Period**: *nothing that did not exist in 1923; flag any word that sounds later.*
- **Dialogue**: *no speech over four lines without an action; no character says another's name twice in a scene.*
- **Translation**: the conventions agreed with your translator.

## Without the AI

Checkers need [the AI reader](/guide/ai-editor/). For mechanical habits you can check without it, **Repetitions** ([[⌃⌘R]]) tints words that come back too soon, and [find and search](/guide/find-and-search/) across the book finds every *suddenly* in a second.
