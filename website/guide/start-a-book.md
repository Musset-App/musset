## What you will have at the end

A book in a folder of your own, a first chapter with two scenes, and a clear idea of where things are. It takes about ten minutes.

## 1. Name the book

Open Musset. On the first screen, or after **File ▸ New Book…** ([[⌘N]]), type a title. A working title will do; you can change it whenever you like.

Three details appear under the title:

- **Genre**: literary, fantasy, crime, romance, essay and others. It sets the columns of the plot grid and the criteria the AI reader would use. It does not constrain your text.
- **Book language**: ten variants, from English (US) to Português (Brasil). It decides the quotation marks and dashes, the spelling checker, the hyphenation and the manuscript format of your country.
- **The folder and the author**: click **Change** to put the book somewhere else or to sign it with another name.

Click **Continue**.

## 2. Choose its form

Musset names and numbers the pieces of the book from this choice:

| Form | Made of |
|---|---|
| **Novel** | Chapters, with scenes inside |
| **Novel in parts** | Parts that group the chapters |
| **Collection** | Stories, essays or poems: titled pieces, without numbers |
| **Journal, letters** | Dated or addressed entries |
| **Essay, document** | Chapters, sections, sub-headings and lists |
| **Play, screenplay** | Acts and scenes |
| **Fragments** | Short blocks, without chapters |
| **Custom** | Levels you name yourself |

Nothing is set in stone. The form can be changed later in the book's settings, and your files do not move.

Click **Begin**. Musset creates the folder, a first chapter, and the book's history.

## 3. Write a paragraph

You are in *Chapter 1*. The line at the top is the chapter's title: select it and type your own.

Now write. A few things happen without your asking:

- your text is saved as you type; there is no Save to remember;
- straight quotes and apostrophes become typographic ones when you leave a paragraph;
- a misspelt word is underlined a moment after you stop.

Press [[↩]] for a new paragraph.

## 4. Start a second scene

At the end of a paragraph, press [[↩]], then [[↩]] again on the empty line. The empty line becomes a scene marker, *Scene 2*, and you keep writing below it. Click its title to give the scene a name of its own.

A third [[↩]] in a row would start a new chapter. So the rhythm of the Return key is the structure of the book: a paragraph, a scene, a chapter.

## 5. Look around

| Where | What is there | Key |
|---|---|---|
| Left | The sidebar: chapters and their scenes, the Bible, drafts | [[⌘1]] |
| Right | Notes, statistics, the plan, the history | [[⌘2]] [[⌘4]] [[⌘6]] [[⌘7]] |
| Top | The switch **Write · Revise · Read**, and Share | |
| Bottom right | The chapter's count and status | |
| Everywhere | The command bar | [[⌘K]] |

Press [[⌘K]] and type a few letters of anything: a chapter's name, *focus*, *export*. Everything in Musset can be reached this way; see [the command bar](/guide/command-bar/).

## 6. Add a chapter and a character

Click **+** at the bottom of the sidebar to add a chapter. It lands at the end of the book; drag it where it belongs.

Then open the **Bible** in the sidebar and create a sheet for your main character: a few lines about who they are. From now on Musset knows the name, and the sheet is one [[⌘K]] away while you write. See [the Bible](/guide/story-bible/).

## 7. See the page

Click **Read** at the top of the window, or press [[⇧⌘R]]. Your chapter appears typeset as a book page, beside the text if the window is wide enough. This is what the PDF will look like.

Press [[⇧⌘R]] again to go back.

## 8. Mark where you are

Your writing is kept in the book's history as you go: every state of the chapter can come back. See [the book's history](/guide/book-history/). To add a point by hand, press [[⌘S]].
