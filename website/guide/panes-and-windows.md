## More than one thing in view

Writing a novel means looking at two things at once: the chapter and the one before it, the scene and the character's sheet, the text and the board. Musset's window splits into **panes**, each with its own **tabs**, and any tab can become a **window** of its own.

Almost anything can be a tab: a chapter, its scene cards, a Bible sheet, a board, the plot grid, the book's overview, the book's history.

![Two chapters side by side](panes)

## Open something beside the page

Three ways, from quickest to most visual:

- **[[⇧⌘O]]**, then type a name. The chapter or sheet opens in the next pane.
- **[[⌘]]-click** it in the sidebar.
- **Drag** it from the sidebar onto the side of the window where you want it: left, right, top or bottom. A highlight shows where it will land.

To split what you are looking at, press [[⌘\]] (**Split Right**) or [[⌥⌘\]] (**Split Down**).

Two panes can show the same chapter. It is one text: what you type in one appears in the other, which is how you keep the opening in view while writing the ending.

## Tabs

A plain click in the sidebar opens a **preview** tab, with its title in italics. The next click replaces it, so browsing ten sheets does not leave ten tabs. A preview becomes a kept tab when you type in it or double-click its title.

[[⌥]]-click in the sidebar opens a kept tab at once. Drag tabs to reorder them, or onto another pane to move them.

| Shortcut | Does |
|---|---|
| [[⌃⇥]] · [[⌃⇧⇥]] | Next · previous tab |
| [[⌥⌘→]] · [[⌥⌘←]] | Next · previous pane |
| [[⌘W]] | Close the tab |
| [[⇧⌘W]] | Close the window |

Drag the line between two panes to resize them. Closing a pane's last tab removes the pane.

## Windows and a second screen

Right-click a tab or a sidebar row and choose **Open in New Window**, or simply drag a tab out of the window. It becomes a window of its own, with its own panes and tabs.

On two screens, a common arrangement is the manuscript in front of you and, on the other screen, a [board](/guide/cork-boards/) that lights up the characters of the paragraph you are writing, with the [Bible](/guide/story-bible/) beside it.

![A board and the Bible on a second screen](second-screen)

Windows left open when you quit come back with the book.

## Save a layout

When an arrangement works, keep it:

1. Choose **Panes ▸ Save the Layout…** and name it: *Drafting*, *Revision*, *Plotting*.
2. Call it back from **Panes ▸ Layouts**, or press [[⌘K]] and type its name.

A few that writers keep:

| Layout | Panes |
|---|---|
| **Drafting** | The chapter alone |
| **Continuity** | The chapter, and the previous chapter beside it |
| **Revision** | The chapter, and its scene cards |
| **Plotting** | The plot grid, and a board |

Layouts belong to the book and to this Mac: your laptop and your desk each keep their own.

## When you want none of it

Press [[⇧⌘D]]. [Focus mode](/guide/distraction-free-writing/) shows only the pane you are writing in, without its tabs, and gives everything back when you leave it.
