## Invite a co-writer

A **co-writer** is the one role that works on the manuscript itself. Readers and editors [read a milestone](/guide/share-with-beta-readers/); a co-writer writes in the text with you and sees the book's documents.

1. Click **Share** in the title bar, then **Invite someone…**.
2. Enter their e-mail or Musset name.
3. Under **What they can do**, choose **Co-writer**.
4. Click **Send the invitation**.

The book has to be online, so you need [Musset Sync](/guide/musset-sync/). Your co-writer signs in, finds the book under **Shared with me** and downloads it.

You can turn an editor into a co-writer later from the list of people. They should send their review first.

## How two people share one text

Musset is not a live document with two cursors. Each of you writes on your own Mac, at your own hours, offline if you like. What each of you writes is sent as you go, and the two are brought together paragraph by paragraph.

- A paragraph only one of you changed is taken as it is.
- A paragraph you both changed keeps **your** version on your Mac, and the other arrives as a [correction](/guide/track-changes/) with your co-writer's name. You accept or reject it.

Nothing asks you a question at the moment it happens, and no text is ever overwritten silently. The status bar says how many paragraphs arrived as corrections.

This suits how books are actually co-written: one of you in the morning on part one, the other at night on part three, and an occasional collision in a shared chapter.

## See who wrote what

Choose **View ▸ Who Wrote What** ([[⌥⌘B]]) with a chapter open.

- The text is tinted, one colour per person.
- A small panel at the bottom left lists each person and their share of the chapter's words.
- Hover over a word to see who wrote it and when.

Musset works this out word by word from the chapter's history. A correction proposed by someone else counts as theirs once you accept it. Fixing a space or an apostrophe does not make a word yours.

It also covers editors who sent corrections [through Word](/guide/word-track-changes/): their accepted changes carry their name.

## Divide the work

A few habits from books written by two:

- **Use statuses as a handshake.** Mark a chapter *Done* when it is ready for the other to read, *Edited* when they have been through it.
- **Leave notes, not e-mails.** A note on a passage ([[⌥⌘N]]) or a conversation stays with the sentence it is about.
- **Set a milestone before a big merge of ideas.** If the experiment fails, the book as it stood is there. See [milestones](/guide/milestones/).
- **Try disagreements in a track.** Each of you can write the contested version in a [track](/guide/tracks/) and compare, with one caution: a given track should be written on one Mac at a time.
