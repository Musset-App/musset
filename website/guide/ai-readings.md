## Ask for a reading

Open the **AI Reader** panel ([[⌘3]]). Above the message field are the readings, one click each:

| Reading | What it looks for |
|---|---|
| **Critique (rubric)** | A scored reading by criteria; see [the rubric critique](/guide/rubric-critique/) |
| **Style & problems** | Repetitions, inconsistencies, clichés, telling where you could show |
| **Tics** | Your own recurring figures, against the book's grid of tics |
| **Continuity** | Contradictions with the other chapters: dates, ages, names, places, a scene told twice |
| **What to cut** | Passages that can go without loss |
| **Opening & ending** | Whether the first paragraph opens the chapter, and the last one lands it |
| **Facts to check** | Names, dates, places and quotations to verify |

Select a passage first and the reading is about that passage only.

The same readings are in the command bar: press [[⌘K]] and type *tics* or *continuity*.

You need the AI reader set up first; see [the editor inside](/guide/ai-editor/).

## What comes back

A short overview in the conversation, a few lines, and the observations themselves **in the margin**: each one a note attached to the passage it is about, with a colour for its kind.

Open the Notes panel ([[⌘2]]) to go through them in the order of the text. Each note says what the reader sees and why. It does not hand you a replacement sentence: that part is yours.

A reading also notes what works. **Strength** notes mark what should survive the revision; they are hidden while you write, and the filter of the Notes panel shows them.

## Work through the notes

For each note you have four moves:

- **Tick it** when you have dealt with it.
- **Resolve** it when you disagree or it does not matter. The reader will not raise that point again.
- **Reply.** Open the thread and ask *why?* or *show me another place where this happens*. **Send** asks the reader to answer in the thread; **Note only** keeps your reply for yourself.
- **Keep** it, to protect it from **Dismiss all…**, which removes everything a reading just left.

See [notes and to-dos](/guide/notes-and-todos/) for the panel itself.

## It does not repeat itself

Each reading is given the notes already in the margin, open and resolved, and is told not to make the same point twice. A point you resolved is settled.

If you have since fixed what an earlier note pointed at, the next reading can resolve that note by itself, with a line saying why.

## Annotate: a narrower reading

In the Notes panel, **Annotate** asks for one kind of note on the chapter or on the selection: **Editorial critique**, **Tics**, **Fact check**, **Continuity**, or **Custom…**, where you say what to look for: *every place where the narrator knows something she could not know*.

## Tics

Every writer has figures they lean on. The **Tics** reading works from a grid you can edit: in the AI Reader panel, open the menu and choose **Tics grid…**. Each family has a description, examples and a test.

The grid Musset proposes was written for French prose: the corrective antithesis, the reversal, the closing line that concludes, the maxim, the burst of fragments, the one-line paragraph for effect. Rewrite it for your language and your own habits; it is a Markdown file kept with the book.

## Tell the reader about your book

If the book's folder holds a file named `AGENTS.md`, its content goes with every request. Use it for what a reader should know: *first person, present tense, British spelling; the narrator is unreliable; no chapter titles.*

## Getting good readings

- **Read the chapter yourself first.** The notes are most useful when they confirm or contradict a doubt you already have.
- **One reading at a time.** *What to cut* and *Opening & ending* on a finished chapter give you a morning's work. All seven give you noise.
- **Use Continuity late**, when the other chapters exist.
- **Treat a note as a question.** The reader is often right that something is wrong there, and sometimes wrong about what.
