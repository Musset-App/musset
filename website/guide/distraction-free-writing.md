## Pick the amount of quiet you need

Some days a clean page is enough. Other days you would switch the internet off at the wall if you could. Musset has a setting for each, and they combine.

| Mode | What it removes | Shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus Mode** | Everything but the sentence and its neighbours | [[⇧⌘D]] |
| **Typewriter Scrolling** | The page moving under your eyes | [[⇧⌘T]] |
| **Kiosk Mode** | The rest of the Mac | [[⌃⌘K]] |
| **Ultra Focus** | The interface, and the way back | [[⌃⌘U]] |

All four are in the **Write** menu. Ultra Focus has [its own page](/guide/ultra-focus/).

## Focus mode

Press [[⇧⌘D]]. The sentence you are writing stays at full strength. The rest of its paragraph fades a little, the paragraphs just before and after fade more, and everything else recedes to a shadow. The tabs and the format bar go too.

When you scroll to reread, the dimming softens so the page is legible; it returns when you type. Musset remembers Focus mode from one day to the next.

## Typewriter scrolling

Press [[⇧⌘T]]. The line you are writing stays in the middle of the window and the text moves up behind it, as paper does in a typewriter. Your eyes stop travelling to the bottom of the screen, and the last line of a chapter is never stuck against the edge.

Clicking somewhere with the mouse leaves the page where it is, so you can still look around.

## Kiosk mode

Press [[⌃⌘K]]. Your Mac becomes a typewriter:

- Musset takes the whole screen, menu bar and notch included;
- the Dock is gone;
- switching to another app with [[⌘Tab]] is off, and so is hiding Musset.

Everything in Musset stays at hand: chapters, notes, the three modes, the command bar. It is only the rest of the computer that is out of reach.

Press [[⌃⌘K]] again to leave. Kiosk mode is never kept when you quit, so Musset cannot reopen locked. And the Mac's own Force Quit ([[⌥⌘Esc]]) always works.

> Kiosk mode is a commitment device, not a prison. It removes the reflex of checking something else, which is usually all that needs removing.

## See repeated words

Press [[⌃⌘R]]. Every word that comes back within fifty words of itself is tinted pink, stronger when the two are closer. Hover over one to frame all its occurrences in the chapter.

Musset groups the forms of a word (*walk*, *walked*, *walking*) and ignores the small words every sentence needs. Nothing is sent anywhere; the count is done on your Mac. The distance is yours to set, from 10 to 300 words, in **Settings ▸ Proofreading ▸ Repetitions**.

The pill at the bottom of the page counts the close repetitions; click it for the list of words.

Repetitions shows and never corrects. Sometimes the echo is the point.

## A map of the chapter, and line numbers

Two more aids live in **Settings ▸ Editor**, and in the command bar ([[⌘K]]):

- **Minimap** draws the whole chapter in a thin column at the right, with marks for notes, search matches and proofreading. Click or drag to move.
- **Line Numbers** numbers the paragraphs in the margin, handy when you discuss a chapter with someone by phone.

## A routine that works

Many writers settle on something like this:

1. Draft in **Ultra Focus** or in Kiosk mode with Focus on: forward only, no rereading.
2. Next morning, reread in plain Write mode with **Repetitions** on.
3. Then switch to **Read** ([[⇧⌘R]]) and see the chapter as a page. See [Write, Revise, Read](/guide/write-revise-read/).
