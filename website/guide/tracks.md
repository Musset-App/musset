## What a track is

Some doubts are bigger than a chapter. What if the father never appears? What if the book is in the present tense? What if part two comes first?

A **track** is a parallel manuscript. You write in it freely, across as many chapters as you like, and the manuscript does not move. Later you bring what works into the manuscript, paragraph by paragraph, or you put the track away.

For a doubt about a single chapter, a [version of the chapter](/guide/chapter-versions/) is lighter.

## Start a track

1. Open the command bar ([[⌘K]]) and choose **New Track…**.
2. Under **What you're trying**, name the idea: *Without the father*.
3. Under **Starting from**, choose the manuscript as it is now, or one of your [milestones](/guide/milestones/).
4. Optionally, start the open chapter on a blank page.
5. Click **Start the Track**.

You can also start from any past moment: in the [history](/guide/book-history/), open a sitting's **…** menu and choose **Start a Track from Here…**.

## Write in a track

While you are in a track, a violet chip in the title bar carries its name, so you always know where your words are going. The manuscript opens beside the editor, read-only, for reference; close it when it distracts you and call it back with **Show the manuscript beside**.

To move between the manuscript and your tracks, open **The Book's History** from the command bar. Under **Where I write**, click the manuscript or a track. What you were writing is saved first.

> A few things belong to the manuscript and wait until you are back in it: creating versions of a chapter, bringing in a Word file, moving chapters to the Trash, and going back in the history. Musset tells you so if you try.

Exporting while in a track exports the track, and its name goes into the file name. That is useful for printing both drafts and reading them on paper.

## Bring what works into the manuscript

1. Open **The Book's History**, right-click the track and choose **Bring into the Manuscript…**.
2. A window opens with three columns: **The manuscript**, **Keep**, and the track. There is one tab for each chapter that differs.
3. For each paragraph that differs, choose **← This one**, **That one →**, or **Both**.
4. Click **Bring into the Manuscript**.

Musset has already chosen for you wherever only one side changed a paragraph. Only paragraphs changed on both sides need your decision, and the main button counts how many are left. **In context** hides the long runs of identical paragraphs.

Two shortcuts help with whole chapters: **Take All from Here** takes the track's chapter as it is, and **Keep the manuscript here** leaves a chapter untouched.

The result is one new point in the manuscript's history. The track stays in the list, marked as brought into the manuscript.

## Put a track away

Right-click the track and choose **Put Away**. It leaves the list and nothing is deleted. If you were writing in it, you return to the manuscript. Tracks you put away stay under the **put away** menu, where you can bring them back.

## On disk

A track is a branch of the book's git history. The files in the book's folder always hold the manuscript, whichever track you are writing in, so another app opening the folder never sees a half-tried idea. Tracks travel with [Musset Sync](/guide/musset-sync/); write in a given track on one Mac at a time.
