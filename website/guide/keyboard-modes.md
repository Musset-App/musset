## Your hands already know a keyboard

Writers are loyal to their keys. Some have used the Mac's shortcuts since the first Word; some still think in WordStar's control diamond, forty years on; programmers who write novels at night have vi or Emacs in their fingers.

Musset does not ask you to change. Open **Settings ▸ Keyboard** and choose one of four modes:

| Mode | In a sentence | Its page |
|---|---|---|
| **Mac / Word** | The shortcuts of every Mac app, and of Word. The default. | [Every shortcut](/guide/keyboard-shortcuts/) |
| **WordStar** | The diamond under the left hand, and blocks that stay marked. | [WordStar keys](/guide/wordstar-keys/) |
| **vi** | Normal, insert and visual modes; Esc belongs to the editor. | [vi keys](/guide/vi-keys/) |
| **Emacs** | Control and Meta chords, the mark and the kill ring. | [Emacs keys](/guide/emacs-keys/) |

One mode is active at a time, for the whole app, and the change takes effect at your next keystroke. The settings page shows a cheat sheet for the mode you pick.

## What is true in every mode

**⌘ stays Musset's.** Whatever the mode, a key pressed with Command does what the menus say: [[⌘K]] opens the command bar, [[⌘S]] saves now, [[⌘F]] finds. The modes take the Control and Option keys, and in vi the plain letters.

**The modes act in the manuscript.** Search fields, sheet titles and note fields behave as on any Mac.

**Edits are ordinary edits.** Anything a mode's command changes can be undone, is tracked as a correction in [Revise mode](/guide/track-changes/), and goes to the Mac's clipboard when it copies or cuts.

**Keys follow the letters.** On an AZERTY, QWERTZ or Dvorak keyboard, a binding is on the key that carries its letter. WordStar's ⌃E is on your E.

**Accents keep working.** While you are composing an accented letter, the keys belong to macOS.

## Motions made for prose

Programmers' editors think in lines. A novelist thinks in words, sentences and paragraphs, and Musset's modes do too:

- a **word** includes its apostrophe, so *don't* and *l'homme* are one word each;
- a **sentence** knows about dialogue: *« Tu viens ? » demanda-t-elle.* is one sentence, not two;
- a **paragraph** is the whole paragraph, however many lines it wraps to on screen.

So in vi, `das` deletes a sentence and `yip` copies a paragraph; in Emacs, [[⌥A]] goes to the start of the sentence.

## Help when you hesitate

Several modes use a prefix: [[⌃K]] and [[⌃Q]] in WordStar, [[⌃X]] and [[Esc]] in Emacs, an operator such as `d` in vi. If you press the prefix and pause for half a second, a card shows what may follow. Turn it off under **Help after a prefix** once your fingers know.

The current state shows at the bottom right of the page: `NORMAL` or `INSERT` in vi, the pending prefix in WordStar and Emacs.

## Which should you choose?

If you have never wondered, stay with **Mac / Word**: it is what you already use everywhere.

Choose **WordStar** if you want to move and edit without leaving the home row and without modes, or if you learned to write on it. Choose **vi** or **Emacs** only if they are already your habit; a novel is a poor place to learn either.

Whatever the mode, the [command bar](/guide/command-bar/) reaches everything else by name.
