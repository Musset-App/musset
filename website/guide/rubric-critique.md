## A score you can argue with

"It's good" teaches nothing, and an AI is quick to say it. The **rubric critique** is built to be useful instead of kind:

- it scores the chapter **criterion by criterion**, against a rubric you set;
- it is **blind**: a separate reader, outside your conversation, who never sees the weights or an earlier score;
- every score must rest on **passages quoted from the chapter**, and Musset checks each quotation against your text. A score whose quotation cannot be found is thrown out.

That last rule matters. A reader that must prove each claim with your own sentence cannot bluff.

## Set the rubric

Choose **Rubric…** at the top of the AI Reader panel ([[⌘3]]), or from the command bar.

| Field | What to put |
|---|---|
| **Start from** | A preset for your genre, or your own grid |
| **What the book promises** | What a reader of this book has been promised: the contract |
| **How demanding** | A known book and what it would score, as a calibration |
| **Your requirements** | What is unforgivable in this book |
| **Threshold** | The score at which you consider a chapter ready |
| **Chapter grid** | The criteria and their weights |

Criteria come from a library in seven families: language, structure, characters, world, ideas, emotion, and what holds the book together. Add the ones that matter for your book and weight them; Musset brings the total to 100.

Fewer is better. With more than ten criteria each weighs little, and Musset warns you when one criterion dominates or another weighs too little to mean anything.

> The presets and the library of criteria were first written in French, for French-language genres, and some labels appear in French. The grid is yours: rename, rewrite and weight the criteria for your book.

## Read a chapter

Click **Critique (rubric)** in the AI Reader panel, or **Read the chapter** on the **AI readings** page.

The reader is given the whole book, so it judges the chapter in its place, along with your synopsis and the Bible. It reads in a session of its own, with no access to your conversation.

If nothing has changed since the last reading, Musset says so and sends nothing.

## What comes back

- **A score out of 100**, with an arrow against the previous reading of the same chapter.
- **An overview** in a few sentences.
- **Up to three axes of work**, each with why, a direction, and concrete steps on quoted passages: *cut*, *move*, *condense*, *develop*.
- **What to keep**, so the revision does not sand off what works.
- **Each criterion** with its score and the passages that justify it.
- **Faults**, such as a cliché, an information dump, an explanatory dialogue, each on its passage.

Reservations, strengths and faults also land in the margin as [notes](/guide/notes-and-todos/), on their passages.

The **AI readings** page keeps a chart of the readings over time, with your threshold as a dashed line, and one bar per chapter for the whole book.

## How the score is made

The reader gives a mark from 0 to 20 for each criterion. Musset does the arithmetic:

- each mark is weighted by the criterion's weight;
- a mark that could not be verified is left out and its weight shared among the others: never counted as zero;
- faults cost points only when their passage is found in the text. No proof, no penalty.

So the number is reproducible from what you can see: the marks, the weights and the quotations.

## How to use it

- **Follow the trend, not the number.** A chapter that goes from 58 to 71 after a revision has improved by this reader's lights. Whether 71 is good depends on how you calibrated.
- **Read the quotations first.** They are where the reading is either right or wrong.
- **Disagree in the margin.** Resolve a note you reject; it will not come back.
- **Compare readers.** With both Claude and ChatGPT set up, read the same chapter with each. Where two readers agree, look closely.
- **Do not write to the rubric.** It measures what you said you wanted. If the book has changed, change the rubric.

## What is sent

A rubric critique sends the text of the whole book, the Bible's sheets, your synopsis and the rubric to your AI provider, under your own account. See [what leaves your Mac](/guide/what-leaves-your-mac/).
