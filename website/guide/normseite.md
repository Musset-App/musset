## What a Normseite is

In German-language publishing the length of a text is given in **Normseiten**, standard pages. A Normseite is a page of **30 lines of at most 60 characters**, spaces included, set in a monospaced face with wide line spacing.

It is the unit publishers and literary agents ask for in submissions, and the unit in which translators and editors are paid. A manuscript sent to a German, Austrian or Swiss house is expected to arrive in Normseiten.

## How it is defined

| Element | Convention |
|---|---|
| Lines per page | 30 |
| Characters per line | 60 at most, spaces included |
| Typeface | Monospaced, usually Courier, 12 point |
| Alignment | Left, ragged right; no hyphenation |
| Paper | A4, single-sided |
| Margins | Wide, with room on the right for the editor's notes |

A full page would hold 30 × 60 = **1,800 characters**. Real pages hold fewer, because paragraphs end mid-line and dialogue leaves short lines: a page of narrative prose usually carries around 1,500 characters. This is why a count by Normseiten and a count by characters never quite agree.

## Two ways of counting

Ask which one is meant, because both are in use:

- **By the page.** Set the text as Normseiten and count the pages. This is the traditional method and the one behind translators' fees.
- **By characters.** Divide the number of characters with spaces by 1,800. This gives a smaller figure, since it treats every page as full.

Musset's title page gives the second: *ca. 274 Normseiten (493.000 Zeichen)*, the characters with spaces divided by 1,800, with the exact count of characters beside it. The number of pages of the PDF itself is the count by the page.

## Make a Normseite manuscript in Musset

1. In the book's settings, set the **Book language** to **Deutsch (Deutschland)** or **Deutsch (Schweiz)**, and enter your **Contact (manuscript)**.
2. Press [[⌘E]] and choose **PDF**.
3. Choose the layout **Manuscript in Normseiten (30 × 60)**.
4. Click **Export**.

Musset sets each page on the grid: 30 lines, 60 characters, Courier 12 point. Every page is filled to its thirty lines, so the page count is honest.

- The title page carries the title, your name, your contact details and the length.
- The header has your surname and the title on the left and the page number on the right.
- Chapters start on a new page, with the number above the title.

## Typography in a German book

While you write, Musset sets German typography as you go: „Anführungszeichen“ with ‚halbe‘ inside, a spaced en dash for the *Gedankenstrich*, and the thin space in *z. B.* and *d. h.* In a Swiss book it uses «Guillemets» and writes ss for ß. See [automatic typography](/guide/smart-typography/).

## Common questions

**How many Normseiten is a novel?** German publishers commonly speak of novels of 250 to 450 Normseiten. What matters is the range the house or the agency states.

**How many words is a Normseite?** German words are long: a Normseite of prose holds roughly 200 to 250 of them. For an English text, count nearer 250 to 300.

**Does the Normseite apply in Austria and Switzerland?** Yes, it is used across the German-speaking countries.

**Must it be Courier?** The grid needs a monospaced face so that each line holds the same number of characters. Courier is the usual one.

Other countries have their own units: see [manuscript formats by country](/guide/manuscript-formats-by-country/).
