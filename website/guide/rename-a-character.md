## Why find and replace is not enough

Renaming Rose to Iris with find and replace has ruined many a manuscript. *Rose* is also a flower and a verb: *the sun rose* becomes *the sun Iris*. And the replace stops at the chapters, leaving the old name in your notes, your character sheets and your outline.

Musset's **Rename** is made for this one job. It changes the name everywhere it means that person, shows you every line before anything changes, and leaves the doubtful cases for you to decide.

## Rename a character or a place

1. Open the character's sheet in the [Bible](/guide/story-bible/) and click **Rename everywhere…**, top right. You can also right-click the sheet in the sidebar and choose **Rename…**, or hover over the name in a chapter and choose **Rename…** on its card.
2. Type the new name.
3. Read the list. Each line shows the passage with the old name struck through and the new one beside it, and the chapter it is in. Untick any line that should stay as it is.
4. Click **Rename**.

The button counts what will change, so you know the size of what you are doing.

## The lines Musset is not sure about

Under **To check**, Musset sets apart the occurrences that may not be your character, each with the reason:

| It says | What it means | Ticked? |
|---|---|---|
| *Also an ordinary word in the book: perhaps not them.* | The name is also a common word in your text, and this occurrence starts a sentence | No |
| *Another sheet has this name too.* | Two sheets share the name | No |
| *Another name for them: choose what it becomes.* | It is one of the character's other names | Yes, once you say what it becomes |

Everything else is under **In the text**, ticked.

So with a character named Rose, *She rose early* is never touched, since it is not capitalised, and *Rose early, she thought…* at the start of a sentence is shown to you unticked.

## Nicknames and other forms

If the sheet lists other ways the character is written, *Rosie*, *Mme Verdier*, each one gets its own line under **Other names**: type what it becomes, or leave it alone.

This is also how you handle forms that a simple match cannot guess, such as a declined form in German or a diminutive: add them to the sheet's **Also written** field first, then rename.

Possessives follow on their own: the *Rose* of *Rose's* is found.

## What is changed

- **The chapters**: every occurrence you left ticked.
- **Your notes**: their text, and the passages they are attached to, so each note still finds its place.
- **The Bible**: the sheet itself, and the name wherever other sheets mention it.
- **The outline**: the plot grid's tags and cells, and the synopsis.

It is saved as a single point in the book's history.

## Undo

The line at the bottom of the window says it: **One undo reverses it all.** Press [[⌘Z]] in the chapter and the chapters, notes, sheets and outline go back as they were.

Later on, the rename is still one entry in [the book's history](/guide/book-history/), and a [milestone](/guide/milestones/) set before a big rename costs nothing.

## Good to know

- Matching respects capitals and whole words: *Rose* does not match *rose* or *Roses*.
- Chapters set aside in Drafts, and the other [versions of a chapter](/guide/chapter-versions/), are not renamed. Rename them by hand with find and replace ([[⌥⌘F]]) if you bring them back.
- Rename is for characters, places and biographies, the sheets Musset tracks in the text. For any other word, use [find and replace](/guide/find-and-search/).
- It works on your Mac, by matching text. No AI is involved.
